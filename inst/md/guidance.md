Incorporation of patient costs in TB modeling analyses 
-----------------------------

- Economic evaluations of TB and HIV services should be designed to reflect the intended use of the results. Studies intended to quantify the welfare implications of intervention options should consider costs borne by intervention recipients and their households (‘patient costs’), in addition to other outcomes.  

- Estimates of patient costs should consider not only the out-of-pocket costs borne by patients and their households, but also the opportunity cost of time associated with the disease and/or receipt of care.  

- To quantify the welfare implications of patient-borne costs, these costs should be compared to measures of the income or wealth of affected households (potentially reported as instances of ‘catastrophic costs’ or ‘impoverishing expenditures’).


Collection of TB patient costs
-----------------------------

If resources are available to collect empirical data on patient costs, methods and instruments should be aligned with existing approaches and guidance in the disease area (for example, [WHO guidance and tools for patient costs during TB disease diagnosis and treatment](https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/9789241513524)). Even if previously-developed instruments are used, investigators should consider whether these should be re-validated for use in the target population and clinical context of interest in the study.
